Section 01 · Points, Lines, Duality & Couples

Hyperbolic Geometry Is Projective Relativistic Geometry

Work over any field F with characteristic not equal to two. A hyperbolic point is a 3-proportion a ≡ [x : y : z] representing a central line through the origin in relativistic space F3; a hyperbolic line is a 3-proportion L ≡ (l : m : n) representing the central plane lx + my − nz = 0.

In the extended Beltrami–Klein view (z = 1), every point maps to [X, Y] = [x/z, y/z] and every line to lX + mY = n. Unlike classical hyperbolic geometry, points outside the unit circle X² + Y² = 1 are treated on equal footing with interior points: the interior models the hyperboloid of two sheets (x² + y² − z² = −1), the unit circle is the null cone, and the exterior models the hyperboloid of one sheet (+1).

Section 02 · Fundamental Metrical Measurements

Quadrance and Spread Replace Distance and Angle

Instead of taking square roots, inverse hyperbolic cosines, or logarithms, universal hyperbolic geometry measures separation between two non-null points a1, a2 by quadrance q(a1, a2), and separation between two non-null lines L1, L2 by spread S(L1, L2). Both are rational functions of the coordinates—and completely dual to one another: q(a1, a2) = S(a1⊥, a2⊥) (Theorem 36).

Definition · Quadrance (Theorem 32)
q(a1, a2) ≡ 1 − (x1x2 + y1y2 − z1z2)² (x1² + y1² − z1²)(x2² + y2² − z2²)

Equals 1 when a1 ⊥ a2, and 0 precisely when a1 = a2 or the join line a1a2 is a null line (Theorem 33). Over ℝ inside the unit disk, q = −sinh²(d).

Definition · Spread (Theorem 34)
S(L1, L2) ≡ 1 − (l1l2 + m1m2 − n1n2)² (l1² + m1² − n1²)(l2² + m2² − n2²)

Equals 1 when L1 ⊥ L2, and 0 precisely when L1 = L2 or the meet point L1L2 is a null point (Theorem 35). Over ℝ at an internal vertex, S = sin²(θ).

Projective Invariance (Theorem 37)
q(a1, a2) = (a1, o2 : a2, o1)

Without logarithms or requiring a1a2 to meet the null circle, quadrance is directly a projective cross-ratio with the opposite points o1 = (a1a2)a1⊥ and o2 = (a1a2)a2⊥.

Section 04 · Null Trigonometry & Universal Constants

When Vertices Lie on the Null Cone: Thinness & the 48/64 Theorem

In classical hyperbolic geometry, ideal triangles have vertices “at infinity” with undefined side lengths. In universal hyperbolic geometry, every null point is explicitly parametrized by a rational proportion via Pythagorean triples: α(t : u) ≡ [t² − u² : 2tu : t² + u²] (Theorem 21), and the join of two null points is L(t1:u1|t2:u2) = (t1t2 − u1u2 : t1u2 + t2u1 : t1t2 + u1u2) (Theorem 23). This unlocks astonishing universal invariants that hold identically for any choice of null points.

Section 05 · Geometry Without Pictures

Exact Hyperbolic Trigonometry Over Finite Prime Fields

Because every definition and law in universal hyperbolic geometry is purely polynomial, the entire theory works over finite prime fields 𝔽p (p > 2). Here, a point [x : y : z] is null if x² + y² − z² ≡ 0 (mod p), external if x² + y² − z² is a non-zero square mod p (lying on 2 null lines), and internal if it is a non-square mod p (lying on 0 null lines).

In Example 17 of the paper, Wildberger constructs the hyperbolic right triangle with spreads 1/4, 1/2, 1 (classical π/6, π/4, π/2) entirely inside 𝔽23 using the three internal points a1 = [14 : 0 : 1], a2 = [0 : 10 : 1], and a3 = [0 : 0 : 1], since 5² ≡ 2 and 7² ≡ 3 (mod 23).
