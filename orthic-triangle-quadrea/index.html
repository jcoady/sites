Section 01 · Core Concept & Live Projective Canvas

Four points on the null conic yield four orthocenters whose triangle quadrea never moves

In N. J. Wildberger’s Universal Hyperbolic Geometry (UHG), hyperbolic trigonometry leaves behind real-number metrics, logarithms, and hyperbolic sines. Instead, it operates algebraically inside the projective plane $\mathbb{P}^2(F)$ over any field $F$ with $\operatorname{char} F \neq 2$, governed by the Lorentzian quadratic form $Q(x, y, z) = x^2 + y^2 - z^2$ and symmetric bilinear form $B(v, w) = x_1 x_2 + y_1 y_2 - z_1 z_2$.

Points satisfying $Q(\alpha) = 0$ are null points—classical points at infinity on the unit circle $X^2 + Y^2 = 1$ in the affine chart $z = 1$. Any rational null point is parametrized stereographically by a proportion $t : u$ via $\alpha(t : u) = [t^2 - u^2 : 2tu : t^2 + u^2]$, with inverse affine parameter $t = \frac{y}{z - x}$.

Cross-Ratio Parameterization

For four distinct null points $\alpha(t_i : 1)$, the projective cross-ratio

$$\lambda = (\alpha_1, \alpha_2 : \alpha_3, \alpha_4) = \frac{(t_1 - t_3)(t_2 - t_4)}{(t_1 - t_4)(t_2 - t_3)}$$

Under $\operatorname{PSL}(2, F)$ symmetry, we fix the canonical gauge $t_1 = -1,\; t_2 = 0,\; t_3 = 1,\; t_4 = \lambda$ ($\lambda \notin \{0, \pm 1\}$), leaving a single free parameter $\lambda$ that governs the entire four-point orbit.

Section 02 · Polar Altitude Construction

How each triply nil triangle generates a well-defined orthocenter via polarity

In classical hyperbolic geometry inside the Beltrami-Klein disk, a triangle’s orthocenter may wander outside the unit disk and vanish from the model. In UHG, every point and line in $\mathbb{P}^2(F)$ remains valid—interior ($Q < 0$), null ($Q = 0$), or exterior ($Q > 0$)—so every triply nil triangle $\alpha_a \alpha_b \alpha_c$ possesses an exact rational orthocenter.

Section 03 · Theorem 4.1 & Algebraic Proof

Why the quadrea collapses to 16/27: degree-24 homogeneous cancellation

For three non-null projective points $a_1, a_2, a_3$, the quadrea $\mathcal{A}(a_1 a_2 a_3)$ is the projective rational analog of $16 \times \text{Area}^2$, defined directly by the squared determinant over the product of vertex quadratic forms:

$$\mathcal{A}(a_1 a_2 a_3) = -\frac{\det([a_1 \;\; a_2 \;\; a_3])^2}{Q(a_1)\,Q(a_2)\,Q(a_3)} = q(a_1, a_3)\,q(a_1, a_2)\,S(a_1 a_2, a_1 a_3)$$

Applying the canonical gauge $\alpha_1 = [0 : -2 : 2]$, $\alpha_2 = [-1 : 0 : 1]$, $\alpha_3 = [0 : 2 : 2]$, and $\alpha_4 = [\lambda^2 - 1 : 2\lambda : \lambda^2 + 1]$, direct join-and-meet computation yields explicit polynomial vectors for the orthocenters $h_1, h_2, h_3$ in terms of $\lambda$:

Polynomial Coordinates in Canonical Gauge
h₁(λ) = [ 8λ(−λ² + 2λ − 1) : 8λ²(1 − λ²) : 8λ(−λ² − 2λ(λ−1)² + 1) ]
h₂(λ) = [ −32λ⁴ + 64λ² − 32 : 128λ(1 − λ²) : 64 − 64λ⁴ ]
h₃(λ) = [ 8λ(λ² + 2λ + 1) : 8λ²(λ² − 1) : 8λ(λ² − 2λ(λ+1)² − 1) ]

Notice that $\det(N)^2$ and the product $Q(h_1)Q(h_2)Q(h_3)$ both expand to degree-24 polynomials in $\lambda$ whose non-constant factors are identical powers of $\lambda$, $(\lambda - 1)$, and $(\lambda + 1)$:

$$\frac{-\det(N)^2}{Q(h_1)Q(h_2)Q(h_3)} = \frac{-2^{26}\,\lambda^8(\lambda-1)^8(\lambda+1)^8}{-27 \cdot 2^{22}\,\lambda^8(\lambda-1)^8(\lambda+1)^8} = \frac{16}{27}$$
