01 · Algebraic Foundation

From Transcendental Angles to the Normalized Gram Invariant

In an ambient 4-dimensional vector space V4 over a field 𝔽 with char(𝔽) ≠ 2, a non-degenerate symmetric bilinear form B(u, v) replaces Euclidean distance and trigonometric sine/cosine functions with purely polynomial measurements. Each non-null vector v ∈ V4 (meaning Q(v) ≡ B(v, v) ≠ 0) represents a projective point a = [v] ∈ ℙ³.

Projective Quadrance & Normalized Gram Volume

Definition 2.1 – 2.2

q(a₁, a₂) = 1 − B(v₁, v₂)² / (Q(v₁)Q(v₂)), Qₖ = det(Gₖ) / ∏ Q(vᵢ)

02 · Volume–Altitude Decomposition

The Quadrume Factorization Theorem: V = Aₖ × hₖ

For any non-degenerate triangular face opposite vertex ak, signed 3×3 cofactors of the rows vjT G construct a unique dual normal vector nk ∈ U⊥ satisfying B(vj, nk) = 0 for all j ≠ k. Orthogonal projection splits vk = vk∥ + vk⊥, reducing the 4×4 Gram determinant via block diagonalization (Lemma 2.6) to det(G4) = det(G3) · Q(vk⊥).

Polynomial Gram Identity & Quadrume Factorization

Theorem 3.3 – 3.4
det(G₄) Q(nₖ) = det(G₃) B(vₖ, nₖ)² ⇒ V = Aₖ × hₖ

Exterior Algebra & Signed Invariants in Indefinite Metrics

In Grassmann algebra over V4, det(Gk) = ⟨v1 ∧ … ∧ vk, v1 ∧ … ∧ vk⟩. Decomposing vk = vk∥ + vk⊥ eliminates the tangential piece inside the wedge product: v1 ∧ … ∧ vk = v1 ∧ … ∧ vk⊥. Unlike Euclidean volume, Qk behaves as a squared hypervolume and naturally takes negative values under Lorentzian signatures (+, +, +, −) without requiring arbitrary absolute values.

03 · Local Vertex Trigonometry

Face Spreads, Vertex Pitches & the Canonical Solid Spread

Around any vertex aj meet three triangular faces (opposite vertices i, k, l). Each face carries a projective face spread Sj,i at aj inside face i, while the altitude from opposite vertex ai defines a vertex pitch Pj,i along edge aiaj. Remarkably, their product eliminates the choice of face i completely.

Solid Spread Identity

Theorem 4.3
Sⱼ = P_{j,i} · S_{j,i} = V / ∏ q(aⱼ, aₓ)

Because hi Ai = V by Quadrume Factorization, all three adjacent faces yield the identical vertex-transitive solid spread Sj.

Projective Solid Spread Law

Theorem 4.6
Sᵢ / ∏ q_opp = V / ∏ q_ab

Dividing each vertex solid spread by the product of its three opposite edge quadrances produces a single global tetrahedron constant.

Alternate Spreads Theorem

Theorem 4.5
S₂,₄/S₃,₄ · S₃,₂/S₄,₂ · S₄,₃/S₂,₃ = 1

Four cyclic products—each omitting one vertex—cancel all face quadreas and edge quadrances identically to 1.

04 · Edge & Dual-Point Geometry

Projective Dihedral Spreads & Cofactor Invariants

Along any edge akal, two triangular faces (opposite vertices ai and aj) meet. Comparing the tetrahedral altitude hi against the in-face altitude hi,j = Aj / qkl yields the projective dihedral spread Ekl. Geometrically, Ekl equals the projective quadrance q(ni, nj) between the dual points of the two meeting faces.

Projective Dihedral Spreads Theorem
