01 · Extending UHG Beyond Three Elements

Closing the Four-Point and Four-Line Gap in UHG

In affine Rational Trigonometry (RT), area-squared invariants are captured by the quadrea of a triangle ($\mathcal{A}$) and extended to quadrilaterals via Bretschneider-type rational identities. In Universal Hyperbolic Geometry (UHG), points and lines are projective 3-proportions $a \equiv [x:y:z]$ and $L \equiv (l:m:n)$ rather than affine vectors. Wildberger established exact laws for triangles and trilaterals—Pythagoras, the Triple Quad Formula (TQF), the Spread Law, the Cross Law, triangle quadrea $\mathcal{A}$, and trilateral quadreal $\mathcal{L}$—yet left the 4-point quadrangle and 4-line quadrilateral unsolved.

Coady’s Theorems 4.1 and 4.2 supply those missing invariants: the quadrea $B(\overline{a_1a_2a_3a_4})$ of a projective quadrangle and its point–line dual, the quadreal $M(\overline{L_1L_2L_3L_4})$ of a projective quadrilateral. Both emerge as the unique common root of two compatible quadratic equations linked to the two triangulations of the four-point figure.

02 · Projective Coordinates, Duality & Triangulations

Points Inside, On, and Outside the Distinguished Circle—and Two Ways to Slice a Quadrangle

In the projective plane over $\mathbb{Q}$, a point $a \equiv [x:y:z]$ represents a central line through the origin in $\mathbb{Q}^3$, projected onto the $z=1$ plane as $[X, Y] = [x/z, y/z]$ when $z \neq 0$. A line $L \equiv (l:m:n)$ represents the plane $lx + my \mp nz = 0$, where the sign is minus in hyperbolic geometry and plus in elliptic geometry.

Unlike the classical Beltrami–Klein disk—which restricts points to the interior $X^2 + Y^2 < 1$ and measures hyperbolic distance via $d = \operatorname{arcsinh}\sqrt{-q}$—UHG operates uniformly across the interior ($q < 0$, hyperboloid of two sheets), the null cone $X^2 + Y^2 = 1$ (points at infinity), and the exterior ($x^2+y^2-z^2 > 0$, hyperboloid of one sheet). Two non-null points $a_1, a_2$ are perpendicular ($a_1 \perp a_2$) precisely when $q(a_1, a_2) = 1$, and dual lines $A_i \equiv a_i^\perp$ obey Quadrance–Spread Duality: $q(a_1, a_2) = S(A_1, A_2)$.

Given a cyclically ordered projective quadrangle $\overline{a_1a_2a_3a_4}$ (no three points collinear), each triangle has quadrea $\mathcal{A}(a_i a_j a_k) = q_{ij}q_{jk}S_j$. Splitting the quadrangle along diagonal $a_1a_3$ produces Triangulation I ($\triangle a_1a_2a_3 + \triangle a_3a_4a_1$), whose quadrea sum is $q_{12}q_{23}S_2 + q_{34}q_{41}S_4$. Splitting along $a_2a_4$ produces Triangulation II ($\triangle a_1a_2a_4 + \triangle a_2a_3a_4$), with sum $q_{41}q_{12}S_1 + q_{23}q_{34}S_3$. Neither sum alone is invariant under cyclic relabeling—yet both anchor a quadratic equation for the true quadrangle quadrea $B$.

Hyperbolic & Elliptic Quadrance / Spread
$$q(a_1, a_2) \equiv 1 - \frac{(x_1x_2 + y_1y_2 \mp z_1z_2)^2}{(x_1^2 + y_1^2 \mp z_1^2)(x_2^2 + y_2^2 \mp z_2^2)}$$

Top sign ($-$): Hyperbolic geometry. Bottom sign ($+$): Elliptic geometry. Replacing $[x_i:y_i:z_i]$ with lines $(l_i:m_i:n_i)$ defines the dual spread $S(L_1, L_2)$.

03 · Archimedes’ Function & Quadratic Compatibility

How Two Quadratic Equations Pin Down One Exact Rational Root

The bridge between triangle quadreas and the quadrangle quadrea $B$ is Archimedes’ function (Wildberger’s Theorem 3.8):

Archimedes’ Function & Triple Quad Formula
$$A(x, y, z) = (x + y + z)^2 - 2(x^2 + y^2 + z^2) = 4xy - (x + y - z)^2$$

In affine geometry, $A(Q_1, Q_2, Q_3) = 0$ is the Triple Quad Formula for collinear points. In algebra, it answers a seemingly unrelated question (Theorem 3.9, Quadratic Compatibility): when do two quadratic equations $(x - p_1)^2 = r_1$ and $(x - p_2)^2 = r_2$ share a common solution?

Computing their resultant shows they are compatible precisely when $A\big(r_1,\, r_2,\, (p_1 - p_2)^2\big) = 0$, or equivalently:

Quadratic Compatibility Condition & Common Root
$$\big((p_1 - p_2)^2 - (r_1 + r_2)\big)^2 - 4r_1r_2 = 0$$
$$p_1 \neq p_2 \implies x = \frac{p_1 + p_2}{2} - \frac{r_1 - r_2}{2(p_1 - p_2)}$$

Notice what happens when we subtract $(x - p_1)^2 = r_1$ and $(x - p_2)^2 = r_2$: the $x^2$ terms cancel! As long as $p_1 \neq p_2$, the unique shared root is a purely linear rational function of $p_1, p_2, r_1, r_2$—requiring no square roots whatsoever.

04 · Main Theorems of the Paper

Theorems 4.1 & 4.2: Quadrea of a Quadrangle and Dual Quadreal of a Quadrilateral

For any non-null projective quadrangle $\overline{a_1a_2a_3a_4}$ with cyclic ordering $(a_1, a_2, a_3, a_4)$, side quadrances $q_{12}, q_{23}, q_{34}, q_{41}$, and vertex spreads $S_1 \equiv S(L_{41}, L_{12})$, $S_2 \equiv S(L_{12}, L_{23})$, $S_3 \equiv S(L_{23}, L_{34})$, $S_4 \equiv S(L_{34}, L_{41})$, Coady proves via symbolic verification of the TQF compatibility identity (Equation 5 of the paper) that both triangulation quadratics always share a solution.

Theorem 4.1 · Projective Quadrangle

Quadrea $B(\overline{a_1a_2a_3a_4})$

The quadrea $B$ is the common solution to the pair of compatible quadratic equations:

$$\big(B - q_{12}q_{23}S_2 - q_{34}q_{41}S_4\big)^2 = 4q_{12}q_{23}q_{34}q_{41}(1-S_1)(1-S_3)$$
$$\big(B - q_{41}q_{12}S_1 - q_{23}q_{34}S_3\big)^2 = 4q_{12}q_{23}q_{34}q_{41}(1-S_2)(1-S_4)$$

Whenever $q_{12}q_{23}S_2 + q_{34}q_{41}S_4 - q_{41}q_{12}S_1 - q_{23}q_{34}S_3 \neq 0$, the unique rational solution is:

$$B = \frac{q_{12}q_{23}S_2 + q_{34}q_{41}S_4 + q_{41}q_{12}S_1 + q_{23}q_{34}S_3}{2} - \frac{4q_{12}q_{23}q_{34}q_{41}\big((1-S_1)(1-S_3) - (1-S_2)(1-S_4)\big)}{2\big(q_{12}q_{23}S_2 + q_{34}q_{41}S_4 - q_{41}q_{12}S_1 - q_{23}q_{34}S_3\big)}$$
Remarks 4.1.1 & 4.1.2 · Symmetry and the Square Case

Cyclic invariance (Remark 4.1.1): Rotating indices $a_1 \to a_2 \to a_3 \to a_4 \to a_1$ simply swaps Equation (3a) with Equation (3b), leaving $B$ unchanged.

Projective square (Remark 4.1.2): When all quadrances equal $q$ and all spreads equal $S$, both equations become $(B - 2q^2S)^2 = \big(2q^2(1-S)\big)^2$, yielding two algebraic solutions: $B = 2q^2$ or $B = 4q^2S - 2q^2$.

Theorem 4.2 · Dual Projective Quadrilateral

Quadreal $M(\overline{L_1L_2L_3L_4})$

By complete point–line duality ($a_i \leftrightarrow L_i$, $q_{ij} \leftrightarrow S_{ij}$, $S_i \leftrightarrow q_i$), the quadreal $M$ of a quadrilateral $\overline{L_1L_2L_3L_4}$ satisfies the dual compatible pair:

$$\big(M - S_{12}S_{23}q_2 - S_{34}S_{41}q_4\big)^2 = 4S_{12}S_{23}S_{34}S_{41}(1-q_1)(1-q_3)$$
$$\big(M - S_{41}S_{12}q_1 - S_{23}S_{34}q_3\big)^2 = 4S_{12}S_{23}S_{34}S_{41}(1-q_2)(1-q_4)$$

And when the centers differ, the closed-form rational quadreal is:

$$M = \frac{S_{12}S_{23}q_2 + S_{34}S_{41}q_4 + S_{41}S_{12}q_1 + S_{23}S_{34}q_3}{2} - \frac{4S_{12}S_{23}S_{34}S_{41}\big((1-q_1)(1-q_3) - (1-q_2)(1-q_4)\big)}{2\big(S_{12}S_{23}q_2 + S_{34}S_{41}q_4 - S_{41}S_{12}q_1 - S_{23}S_{34}q_3\big)}$$
Quadreal of a Quadrangle & Quadrea of a Quadrilateral

Consistent with triangle–trilateral convention, the quadreal $M(\overline{a_1a_2a_3a_4})$ of a quadrangle is the quadreal of its associated quadrilateral $L_{12}L_{23}L_{34}L_{41}$ (obtained by swapping $q_{ij} \leftrightarrow S_i$ cyclically in Theorem 4.2).

07 · Universal Reach & Applied Domains

One Algebraic Structure Across Relativistic Minkowski Space and Celestial Spheres

Remark 4.1.3 highlights one of the deepest insights of Universal Hyperbolic Geometry: the polynomial equations of Theorems 4.1 and 4.2 do not depend on whether the ambient three-dimensional quadratic form is hyperbolic ($x^2+y^2-z^2$) or elliptic ($x^2+y^2+z^2$). Because all proofs proceed purely through rational polynomial identities over $\mathbb{Q}$, every theorem in this framework applies simultaneously to Lorentzian spacetime geometry and spherical celestial trigonometry.

Relativistic Physics

Projective Spacetime & Null Cones

Hyperbolic geometry is precisely projective relativistic geometry. The unit circle $X^2+Y^2=1$ is the null light cone ($x^2+y^2-z^2=0$); interior points form the hyperboloid of two sheets (timelike rays), while exterior points form the hyperboloid of one sheet (spacelike rays).
