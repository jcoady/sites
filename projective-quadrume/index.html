01 · Algebraic Foundations

Why projective volume belongs to polynomial algebra, not calculus

Classical non-Euclidean geometry measures a tetrahedron using real coordinates, transcendental functions such as \(\sinh x\) and \(\cos \theta\), and square roots that force numerical approximations. Building on N. J. Wildberger’s rational trigonometry and universal hyperbolic geometry, John D. Coady replaces distance and angle with purely algebraic invariants defined over any field \(\mathbb{F}\) of characteristic not equal to \(2\), equipped with a non-degenerate symmetric bilinear form \(B(u, v)\).

Keeping \(\operatorname{char}(\mathbb{F}) \neq 2\) guarantees that \(2\) remains invertible and that the symmetric bilinear form does not collapse into an alternating form. Within an \(n\)-dimensional ambient vector space \(V_n\), every non-null vector \(v \in V_n\) defines a projective point \(a = [v] \in \mathbb{P}^{n-1}\), and its fundamental self-measurement is its vector quadrance \(Q(v) \equiv B(v, v) \neq 0\).

Definitions 2.1 – 2.3 · Projective Gram Volume Generalizes \(k = 2, 3, 4, \dots, n\)
\[ q(a_1, a_2) \equiv 1 - \frac{B(v_1, v_2)^2}{Q(v_1)\,Q(v_2)} \qquad\text{and}\qquad \mathcal{Q}_k \equiv \frac{\det(G_k)}{\prod_{i=1}^{k} Q(v_i)} \quad\text{with}\quad [G_k]_{ij} = B(v_i, v_j) \]
Scale Invariance (Proposition 2.4) Scaling any representative \(v_i \to \lambda_i v_i\) multiplies row \(i\) and column \(i\) of \(G_k\) by \(\lambda_i\), scaling \(\det(G_k)\) by \(\prod \lambda_i^2\). The denominator \(\prod Q(\lambda_i v_i)\) scales by the exact same \(\prod \lambda_i^2\) and cancels identically.
Null Vectors Excluded A vector \(v\) is null when \(Q(v) = 0\). Because projective normalization divides by \(Q(v_i)\), projective points in the factorization are represented by non-null vectors.

02 · The Core Formula

The projective quadrume \(\mathcal{V}\) of a tetrahedron in \(\mathbb{P}^3\)

Let \(a_1, a_2, a_3, a_4\) be four non-coplanar projective points in \(\mathbb{P}^3\), represented by non-null vectors \(v_1, v_2, v_3, v_4\) in the four-dimensional vector space \(V_4\). Writing the pairwise bilinear products as \(B(v_i, v_j)\) and the self-quadrances as \(Q(v_i) = B(v_i, v_i)\), the projective quadrume \(\mathcal{V}\) is the normalized \(4 \times 4\) Gram determinant:

Definition 3.1 · Projective Quadrume Formula Equation (7) in Coady (2026)
\[ \mathcal{V}(a_1, a_2, a_3, a_4) \equiv \frac{\det(G_4)}{Q(v_1)\,Q(v_2)\,Q(v_3)\,Q(v_4)} = \frac{1}{\prod_{i=1}^{4} B(v_i, v_i)} \det \begin{pmatrix} B(v_1,v_1) & B(v_1,v_2) & B(v_1,v_3) & B(v_1,v_4) \\ B(v_2,v_1) & B(v_2,v_2) & B(v_2,v_3) & B(v_2,v_4) \\ B(v_3,v_1) & B(v_3,v_2) & B(v_3,v_3) & B(v_3,v_4) \\ B(v_4,v_1) & B(v_4,v_2) & B(v_4,v_3) & B(v_4,v_4) \end{pmatrix} \]
Squared Hypervolume & Sign (Remark 4.5) Because \(\mathcal{V}\) is built from a Gram determinant, it behaves like a squared volume. In Euclidean signature \((+,+,+,+)\), \(\mathcal{V} \ge 0\) and \(\sqrt{|\mathcal{V}|}\) is proportional to normalized Euclidean tetrahedral volume (via Cayley–Menger). In indefinite Lorentzian signature \((+,+,+,-)\), \(\mathcal{V}\) can be negative without taking absolute values.
Exterior Algebra View (Remark 2.5) In the fourth exterior power \(\bigwedge^4 V_4\), the numerator equals the squared norm \(\langle v_1 \wedge v_2 \wedge v_3 \wedge v_4,\, v_1 \wedge v_2 \wedge v_3 \wedge v_4 \rangle\). Only the component of \(v_4\) orthogonal to \(\operatorname{span}(v_1, v_2, v_3)\) survives the wedge product.
Complete Vertex Symmetry Swapping any two vertices exchanges two rows and two columns of \(G_4\), leaving \(\det(G_4)\) unchanged. Consequently, \(\mathcal{V}\) is symmetric across all four faces—even though each face yields a different pair \((\mathcal{S}, h)\).

03 · The Factorization Theorem

Why \(\mathcal{V}\) splits cleanly into face quadrea times altitude quadrance

In elementary geometry, the volume of a tetrahedron equals one-third of its base area multiplied by its perpendicular height. Coady proves an exact algebraic counterpart in \(\mathbb{P}^3\)—with no factor of \(1/3\), no square roots, and no reliance on real numbers.

Pick three vertices \(a_1, a_2, a_3\) spanning a three-dimensional subspace \(U = \operatorname{span}(v_1, v_2, v_3) \subset V_4\) on which the bilinear form \(B|_U\) is non-degenerate. Their normalized \(3 \times 3\) Gram determinant defines the face quadrea \(\mathcal{S}\) of triangle \(a_1 a_2 a_3\).

Next, construct the unique projective point \(n \in \mathbb{P}^3\) dual to the plane \(a_1 a_2 a_3\), meaning \(B(n, v_i) = 0\) for \(i \in \{1, 2, 3\}\). Measuring the separation between the fourth vertex \(a_4\) and the dual point \(n\) gives the projective altitude quadrance \(h(a_4)\). Multiplying the two recovers the projective quadrume \(\mathcal{V}\) identically.

Geometric anatomy of the factorization

Schematic representation in affine chart; orthogonal complement \(U^\perp\) is defined with respect to \(B\).

Theorem 3.3 & Corollary 3.4 · Identity & Factorization Equations (8)–(10) in Coady (2026)
\[ \det(G_4)\, Q(n) = \det(G_3)\, B(v_4, n)^2 \qquad\Longrightarrow\qquad \mathcal{V} = \mathcal{S} \times h(a_4) \quad\text{where}\quad h(a_4) \equiv 1 - q(a_4, n) = \frac{B(v_4, n)^2}{Q(v_4)\, Q(n)} \]

Step-by-step derivation from cofactors to congruence

STEP 01

Construct the dual point \(n\) via signed cofactors (Proposition 2.7)

Represent the bilinear form \(B\) by the symmetric \(4\times 4\) matrix \(G\), and assemble the \(3\times 4\) matrix \(M\) whose rows are \(v_1^T G,\ v_2^T G,\ v_3^T G\). Deleting column \(i \in \{0,1,2,3\}\) yields the \(3\times 3\) minor \(M_{\hat{i}}\), and the dual vector \(n\) has entries \(n_i = (-1)^i \det(M_{\hat{i}})\). By Laplace expansion, \(B(v_k, n) = v_k^T G n = 0\) for all \(k \in \{1, 2, 3\}\). Because \(B|_U\) and \(B\) are non-degenerate, the orthogonal complement \(U^\perp = \operatorname{span}(n)\) is non-degenerate, forcing \(Q(n) = B(n, n) \neq 0\).

STEP 02

Orthogonally project \(v_4\) onto \(U^\perp\) and block-diagonalize \(G_4\) (Lemma 2.6)

Under the direct sum \(V_4 = U \oplus U^\perp\), split \(v_4 = v_{4\parallel} + v_{4\perp}\) with \(v_{4\parallel} \in U\) and \(v_{4\perp} = \frac{B(v_4, n)}{Q(n)}\, n \in U^\perp\). Subtracting the linear combination \(v_{4\parallel} = \sum_{i=1}^3 \mu_i v_i\) via an upper-triangular transition matrix \(P\) with \(\det(P) = 1\) diagonalizes the Gram matrix:

\[ G'_4 = P G_4 P^T = \begin{pmatrix} G_3 & \mathbf{0} \\ \mathbf{0}^T & Q(v_{4\perp}) \end{pmatrix} \quad\Longrightarrow\quad \det(G_4) = \det(G_3) \cdot Q(v_{4\perp}) = \det(G_3)\,\frac{B(v_4, n)^2}{Q(n)} \]
STEP 03

Establish the Polynomial Gram Identity and normalize

Multiplying by \(Q(n)\) produces \(\det(G_4)\,Q(n) = \det(G_3)\,B(v_4, n)^2\). Since both sides are polynomials in the 16 vertex coordinates and agree on the Zariski-dense set where \(B|_U\) is non-degenerate, the polynomial identity holds identically for all configurations—even degenerate ones. Whenever \(Q(v_i) \neq 0\) and \(Q(n) \neq 0\), dividing both sides by \(Q(v_1)Q(v_2)Q(v_3)Q(v_4)Q(n)\) yields \(\mathcal{V} = \mathcal{S} \times h(a_4)\).

05 · Higher-Dimensional Simplices

A universal recursion from \(\mathbb{P}^1\) to \(\mathbb{P}^{n-1}\)

The tetrahedron factorization \(\mathcal{V} = \mathcal{S} \times h(a_4)\) is the \(k = 4\) instance of a general theorem holding for any \(k\) linearly independent projective points \(a_1, \dots, a_k\) in \(\mathbb{P}^{n-1}\) (\(k \le n\)). Whenever the bilinear form restricted to the \((k-1)\)-dimensional face span \(U_{k-1} = \operatorname{span}(v_1, \dots, v_{k-1})\) is non-degenerate, its orthogonal complement inside \(U_k = \operatorname{span}(v_1, \dots, v_k)\) is one-dimensional and spanned by a unique dual normal vector \(n \in U_k\).

Theorem 4.2 & Remark 4.3 · Recursive Factorization Equations (11)–(17) in Coady (2026)
\[ \mathcal{Q}_k = \mathcal{Q}_{k-1} \times h(a_k) \qquad\text{and if every flag subspace } U_1 \subset \dots \subset U_{k-1} \text{ is non-degenerate:}\qquad \mathcal{Q}_k = \prod_{j=2}^{k} h(a_j) \]

k = 3 Triangle (2-simplex) \(\mathbb{P}^2 \subset \mathbb{P}^{n-1}\) Projective quadrea \(\mathcal{S}\) \(\mathcal{S} = q(a_1, a_2) \times h(a_3)\)

Boundary and degeneracy conditions (Proposition 4.4)

Even though the polynomial determinant relation \(\det(G_k)\,Q(n) = \det(G_{k-1})\,B(v_k, n)^2\) holds for every coordinate tuple, the normalized projective factorization \(\mathcal{Q}_k = \mathcal{Q}_{k-1} \times h(a_k)\) becomes undefined in two distinct situations:

  • Null vertex: At least one representative vector is null (\(Q(v_i) = 0\)), which sets the projective denominator to zero.
  • Degenerate base span: \(\det(G_{k-1}) = 0\), which occurs either when \(a_1, \dots, a_{k-1}\) are linearly dependent, or when they are independent in an indefinite metric yet \(B|_{U_{k-1}}\) is degenerate (\(U_{k-1} \cap U_{k-1}^\perp \neq \{0\}\)), forcing \(Q(n) = 0\).

06 · Symbolic Verification

Independent proof in the 16-variable polynomial ring

Because any symmetric bilinear form over a field of characteristic not \(2\) can be diagonalized, Coady confirmed Theorem 3.3 symbolically in SymPy over the polynomial ring \(\mathbb{F}[a_0, \dots, d_3]\) using a general diagonal metric \(G = \operatorname{diag}(g_0, g_1, g_2, g_3)\). Expanding \(\Delta = \det(G_4)\,Q(n) - \det(G_3)\,B(v_4, n)^2\) reduces identically to \(0\).

View SymPy verification script (Appendix A of Coady 2026)
Python 3 · SymPy exact polynomial verification 
import sympy as sp

def run_symbolic_proof():
    A = list(sp.symbols('a0:4'))
    B_pt = list(sp.symbols('b0:4'))
    C = list(sp.symbols('c0:4'))
    D = list(sp.symbols('d0:4'))

    g0, g1, g2, g3 = sp.symbols('g0 g1 g2 g3')
    G = sp.diag(g0, g1, g2, g3)

    def B_form(u, v):
        return (sp.Matrix(u).T * G * sp.Matrix(v))[0, 0]

    def symbolic_dual_point(A, B_pt, C):
        M = sp.Matrix([
            list(sp.Matrix(A).T * G),
            list(sp.Matrix(B_pt).T * G),
            list(sp.Matrix(C).T * G)
        ])
        n = []
        for i in range(4):
            M_sub = M.copy()
            M_sub.col_del(i)
            n.append(((-1)**i) * M_sub.det())
        return n

    pts = [A, B_pt, C, D]
    G4 = sp.Matrix([[B_form(u, v) for v in pts] for u in pts])
    G3 = G4[:3, :3]

    n = symbolic_dual_point(A, B_pt, C)
    LHS = G4.det() * B_form(n, n)
    RHS = G3.det() * (B_form(D, n) ** 2)

    difference = sp.simplify(LHS - RHS)
    print("Symbolic Difference (LHS - RHS) =", difference)
    print("Is the identity proved?", difference == 0)

if __name__ == "__main__":
    run_symbolic_proof()
